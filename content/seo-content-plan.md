# datings.lol — 100-Article SEO Content Plan

**Status:** strategy and editorial brief (batch 1: plan only)  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-26  
**Site analyzed:** `datings.lol` is a playful, brutalist dating-coach and chat-roaster app. Its current product surface includes daily lessons, AI chat coaching/roasts, profile audits, streaks, journaling, community teaching, and a paid Pro path. The product voice is candid and funny, but the content must remain kind, inclusive, practical, and safety-conscious.

## Audience and positioning

- **Core audience:** adults 18+ using dating apps who want better profiles, more natural conversations, stronger confidence, and safer dates; especially beginners who dislike generic dating advice.
- **Secondary audiences:** people returning to dating, neurodivergent daters looking for explicit scripts and structure, LGBTQ+ daters seeking adaptable guidance, and intermediate users who want to diagnose low matches or stalled chats.
- **Jobs to be done:** improve a profile, choose photos, write or answer messages, move from chat to a date, recover from awkward moments, set boundaries, and understand app mechanics without pretending there is a magic line.
- **Editorial promise:** specific examples, decision frameworks, scripts with context, consent and privacy defaults, and honest trade-offs. Never guarantee matches or present manipulation as confidence.
- **Existing-content gap:** the app has product content and short lessons, but no discoverable evergreen library. These pages create an SEO entry point into the coach, profile audit, daily lessons, and community features.

## Search and information architecture

The plan uses ten clusters. Pillar pages (01, 11, 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71, 81, 91) should link to their supporting pages; supporting pages link back to the pillar and to the next logical action. Use descriptive contextual links rather than a repeated “click here.” Add breadcrumbs, Article/FAQ schema only when the visible page contains the marked-up content, author/date/review information, and a concise “last reviewed” date for safety or platform-specific pages.

**Editorial rules:** one primary intent per URL; answer the query in the first 100 words; use examples instead of filler; refresh app-specific facts quarterly; avoid gender stereotypes, “alpha” framing, fake urgency, and promises of outcomes. Meta titles/descriptions below are drafts and should be checked in the CMS for pixel width.

1. **Launch the ten pillars first:** 01, 07, 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71, 81, and 91. Add 100 as the navigation hub once the first clusters are live.

3. **Cross-link by journey:** profile pages link to messaging; messaging links to dates; dates link to safety and relationships; confidence pages link back to practice; safety pages link to official resources and never rely on a product CTA as the only next step.
4. **Use article IDs in the editorial calendar, not in live copy:** IDs are stable planning references. Live anchors should describe the destination, such as “how to choose a first date” or “dating profile audit.”
5. **Update cadence:** review platform-specific, pricing, privacy, scam, and reporting pages every quarter; review evergreen advice every six months; log expert and lived-experience reviewers on sensitive topics.
6. **Measurement:** track impressions and clicks by cluster, non-branded entrances, assisted signups, scroll depth, internal-link clicks, and content-assisted coach starts. Do not use match count as the sole success metric.
